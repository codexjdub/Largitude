Largitude shows the full-size version of an image when you rest the mouse on it: a thumbnail, a picture in a post, or a link to an image file. Videos play bigger too: links to video files, Imgur GIFVs, X GIFs, Giphy GIFs, small videos on a page, and Reddit's videos, with their sound. The popup opens beside the pointer, fitted to the window, and closes when you move away.

• Finds the largest version a page offers: a link to the image file, the biggest of the sizes the page lists, or the image itself.
• Knows where many sites keep the original uploads, so you see the full image rather than the page's smaller copy: Reddit, X, Bluesky, Imgur, Wikipedia, Amazon, IMDb, Pinterest, eBay and Flickr, and any site whose images come from WordPress, Next.js, Cloudinary, imgix or Google.
• Galleries: on a Reddit gallery, or an X or Bluesky post with several images, the arrow keys or the mouse wheel move through them, with a counter showing where you are.
• Videos play muted and looping, with a bar showing how far they've played. M turns the sound on, Space pauses.
• Keys while it's open: S saves the image or video, O opens it in a new tab, C copies its address, and Z shows an image at its actual size, the mouse moving around it.
• YouTube: a video's largest still, for its thumbnails and links to it on other sites.
• Sees through the transparent layers many sites put over their images, and into web components.
• Opens at its final size right away, with the thumbnail standing in while the full image loads.
• Stays out of the way: the mouse goes right through it, it skips images already at full size and small icons, and a click, Escape or scrolling closes it.
• Turn it off on a site with its toolbar button, or hold Shift to keep popups away for a moment.
• Settings: sites to leave alone, and which way the wheel moves through a gallery. Changes apply at once.

Largitude collects no data and sends nothing anywhere. It only loads the images and videos the page refers to, or larger versions of them at addresses worked out from those.
